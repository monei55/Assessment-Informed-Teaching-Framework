# The Professional Reasoning Model

## Student Success Sits at the Centre

Every instructional decision influences a student’s opportunity to learn and succeed.

The Assessment Informed Teaching Framework makes the professional reasoning behind those decisions visible so it can be learned, practised and refined.

---

## The Connected System

The framework connects eight interdependent components:

1. Curriculum Intent
2. Achievement Standards
3. Assessment Design
4. Marking Guide
5. Teaching Decisions
6. Visible Learning
7. Evidence of Learning
8. Moderation

These components do not operate in isolation.

When one component becomes clearer, the decisions connected to it become stronger. When one component is misunderstood or overlooked, the effects continue through the teaching and learning process.

---

## The Four Clarity Lenses

The connected system is examined through four professional lenses.

### Learning Clarity

**What are students expected to learn?**

Understand the curriculum intent, essential learning and cognitive demand.

### Assessment Clarity

**How will students demonstrate successful learning?**

Understand what quality looks like and ensure students have genuine opportunities to demonstrate it.

### Pedagogical Clarity

**What will best support this cohort to succeed?**

Design responsive learning experiences that maintain high expectations and address diverse needs.

### Impact Clarity

**What is the evidence telling us?**

Interpret evidence, evaluate impact and determine the next instructional response.

---

## The Purpose

The framework does not provide educators with fixed answers.

It develops the professional reasoning required to make sound decisions for a particular curriculum, assessment, cohort and context.

Educators should become increasingly confident and less dependent on others to:

- unpack curriculum
- interpret achievement standards
- analyse assessment tasks
- understand marking guides
- design accessible assessment
- plan responsive teaching
- make learning and quality visible
- interpret evidence
- moderate judgements
- determine next instructional steps

---

## Professional Capability Develops Through Practice

The first cycle may feel demanding because the reasoning is deliberate and visible.

With repeated use, educators become more efficient at recognising connections, identifying misalignment and making defensible instructional decisions.

The process gradually becomes part of normal professional practice.

> **Professional capability grows through professional reasoning.**

## The Student Experience

When the connected system is working effectively, students are more likely to understand:

- what they are learning
- why the learning matters
- what successful learning looks like
- where they are currently performing
- what they need to do next
- how to improve the quality of their work

Learning walls, success criteria, exemplars and bump-it-up walls make this clarity visible, but their value depends on the professional reasoning behind them.

---

## The Leadership Role

Leadership teams need a strong understanding of the same reasoning process so they can:

- guide teachers through planning
- strengthen assessment literacy
- ask purposeful coaching questions
- lead before-moderation conversations
- support consistent professional judgements
- identify where capability needs to be strengthened
- connect Department resources to the point where they are most useful

Leadership should build professional capability rather than reduce the process to compliance.

---

## Framework Principle

> **If one part of the connected system lacks clarity, it can reduce students’ opportunities to demonstrate successful learning.**

The goal is not perfection in every component.

The goal is an increasingly coherent system in which educators understand how their decisions connect and can identify where further reasoning or support is required.
